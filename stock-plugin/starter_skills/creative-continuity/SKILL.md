# Creative continuity

Determine whether this turn continues established material, revises it, or explores alternatives. Use the user's current direction when it intentionally changes earlier canon.

Maintain only the relevant continuity anchors: characters' motivations and knowledge, relationships, setting rules, current location/time, unresolved promises and voice. Distinguish user-approved canon, draft options, in-character beliefs and narrator facts. A character can be wrong without the world becoming inconsistent.

Retrieve the smallest relevant source passages rather than loading an entire history. If a missing fact matters, ask a focused question or explicitly offer an optional assumption. Do not silently establish major lore, relationships or character choices on the user's behalf.

For multiple characters, track who knows what and preserve distinct goals and voices. Check new scenes for causal and emotional continuity, not only matching names. Keep revision notes separate from the creative output so future recall does not mistake rejected alternatives for canon.

Personal information used in a fictional scene is not automatically a fact about the user. This skill does not authorize storing, sharing or diagnosing personal traits from roleplay.
